3

1. Profile optimisation

Before you chase reach, fix what people see when they click you: your profile. Photo, banner, and bio should answer in seconds: who is this, what do they stand for, and why follow.

Checklist:

  • Profile image (avatar) — Your face, plain and simple: well lit, in focus, easy to recognise at thumbnail size. Not a logo, not a tiny figure in a crowd. You can smile or stay serious; either works if your face is clearly visible.
  • Cover image (banner) — Something you offer or promise: a result people want, a problem you own, or a reason they should follow you. One bold idea; no wallpaper noise.
  • Bio — use the formula on the following pages; add proof last.

Examples for photo and banner are on the next page.

4

Profile image & cover

a) Profile image — your face, clearly

b) Cover image — your offer / reason to follow

GET PAID TO WRITE ONLINE

Quick rules

  • Avatar: Must be your face — close enough that people see you clearly (eyes, expression). Good light, simple background. Smile or serious is fine; avoid hiding behind hats, dark shadows, or busy crops.
  • Cover: Say what you offer or what people gain by following you — a result, a belief, or a clear theme. Make it something they want, not decoration. Huge text, one idea, readable on mobile.

Bio — the formula

Build your headline and about section around:

I help X doing Y with Z
  • X — who (niche / segment)
  • Y — outcome they want
  • Z — your mechanisms (how)

Writing simplified

Writing on social is simple (not easy).

You only need two things.

Your writing has to look good and be useful.

You make it useful with the content goals:

  • Education
  • Inspiration
  • Entertainment

You make it look good with aesthetic writing — easy to consume. It includes (but isn’t limited to):

  • Rule of 3
  • Simplicity
  • Stairways
  • Polarizing
  • Repetition
  • Bullet points
  • White space
  • Write as you speak

8

Rule of 3

The rule of 3 improves rhythm. More clarity, more persuasive, more memorable. It’s usually stronger than one or two items — use 3s in lists when you can.

Example:

✓
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian
Most founders: • Chase vanity • Copy trends • Ignore positioning Me: • Niche down • Ship proof • Own one story Pick a lane.
✕
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian
Most founders: • Chase vanity • Ignore positioning Me: • Niche down • Ship proof • Own one story • Post daily Pick a lane.

9

Simplicity

Don’t use more words when fewer will do. People are busy. Write like it.

  • Simple
  • Concise
  • Practical

Simplicity reads like confidence.

Example:

✓
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian
You don’t need more tactics. You need one offer people understand.
✕
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian
You don’t necessarily need to keep adding tactics if you’re feeling overwhelmed; what you probably need is to simplify your offer so prospects actually understand what you do and why it matters.

White space

White space is incredibly important.

  • Easier to read
  • Easier to intrigue
  • Easier to understand

It lowers the perceived effort to read your post — so more people finish it. Add space after short lines.

Example:

✓
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian
I’ll be happy once I ship the launch. *Ships launch* Still anxious. Why? The next milestone won’t fix your habits.
✕
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian

15

Write as you speak · sentence length

You should write as you speak. Beginners try to sound smart — it backfires. If you wouldn’t say it out loud, don’t type it.

Vary sentence length. Short. Then a longer line that carries one clear idea — then short again. Monotone length feels like a textbook.

Example:

✓
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian
You don’t need a perfect plan. You need to post for 90 days and iterate.
✕
Andrei Lucian @theandreilucian
One does not require an elaborate multi-quarter strategic roadmap prior to commencement of organic distribution efforts; one requires a sustained publishing cadence over a finite horizon.

Practical gameplan

Batch hooks on Sunday. Ship 3–5 posts per week. Protect one engagement block daily.

Day Content Engage DMs
Mon Draft / schedule 2 posts; pick templates 60 min (split: yours + niche feed) 5–10 thoughtful
Tue Publish + one comment capture / story 60 min 5–10
Wed How-to or framework post 60 min 5–10
Thu Proof or opinion 60 min 5–10
Fri Review saves / replies; steal your own format 45 min Optional
Sat–Sun Batch next week’s hooks; optional carousel Light / off Off

Adjust to your bandwidth — keep the structure, not every number.

Content strategy & templates

Rotate formats so you never run out of ideas. Pillar mix: proof, opinion, how-to. Every post should make one idea stick.

Templates 1–5

  1. Mistake → fix
  2. List breakdown (3–5 bullets)
  3. Story → lesson
  4. Contrarian hook + nuance
  5. Before / after

Templates 6–10

  1. Carousel / document
  2. DM prompt (sparingly)
  3. Comment capture + answer
  4. Founder diary
  5. Resource drop

Cadence: e.g. Mon opinion · Wed how-to · Fri story — tune to you.

Engagement game

60 minutes per day — budget this for real interaction (not doom-scrolling).

A) Reply to people who engage on your posts. Don’t answer with one word like “yes”, “ye”, or “indeed” — use at least one full sentence, and add a question or next step when it fits.

22

DMs & networking

“Your network is your net worth.”

Most people lead with taking. Flip it: curiosity → value → optional call. Target 20–30 real DMs per day only to people you’d actually talk to.

Mindset

  • Genuine interest in their work; value before asks
  • Reference something specific they posted
  • OK being ignored — not a verdict on you

Avoid “let’s connect” with no context, needy tone, “hi” with no hook, hollow reciprocity, giant voice notes.

23

DM openers & depth

Steps 1–3 — Find people through comments; follow + warm up; first message = why their post/bio hit (short).

Hey [name] — your [post/bio] landed because [specific reason]. Optional: one question.

Inspiration, not 30 copy-pastes.

Steps 4–5 — Ask about what they’re building; offer a useful note or intro when it’s real (not manipulation).

Steps 6–7 — Short call if values align; move great fits to Telegram / email. Skip hollow pods.

24

Creators, rhythm & today

Small vs big creators: bigger inboxes need a sharper reason to reply. Lead with value; stay non-needy; treat them as peers, not idols.

Weekly rhythm

  • Mon — draft/schedule posts; refresh bio if needed
  • Tue–Thu — engagement + outbound DMs
  • Fri — review what earned saves/replies
  • Weekend — optional long-form or carousel

Signals you’re winning: strangers cite your themes; DMs get specific; you can predict saves vs comments.

Today

  1. Photo, banner, bio (formula)
  2. Schedule 3 posts from your templates
  3. 60 min: comments + replies
  4. 5 thoughtful DMs

If you’re stuck

  • Low views? Fix the first two lines (hook).
  • No DMs? Proof + CTA in bio; end posts with a question.
  • Burnout? One post/day + keep the engagement block sacred.

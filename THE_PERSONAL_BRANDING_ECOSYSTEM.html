PART 2: THE WRITING SYSTEM

How to Write with Impact (Not Perfection)

Forget everything you learned about "good writing."

Digital writing is different.

The Shift:

  • From: Perfect grammar, complex sentences
  • To: Clear value, simple language, impact

The Framework:

  1. Start with the Problem:

    • What problem are you solving?
    • Why does this matter?
    • Who needs this?

  2. Deliver Value Quickly:

    • Don't bury the insight
    • Lead with the takeaway
    • Make it scannable

  3. Use Simple Language:

    • Short sentences
    • Clear words
    • No jargon (unless necessary)

  4. Show, Don't Just Tell:

    • Examples
    • Case studies
    • Real experiences

  5. End with Action:

    • What should they do?
    • Question to engage?
    • Next step?

Example Transformation:

Before (Academic Writing): "In the contemporary digital landscape, the proliferation of content creation methodologies has engendered a paradigm shift in how individuals and organizations approach the dissemination of information."

After (Impact Writing): "Most creators waste hours creating content.

Here's a better way:

Write 1 newsletter article, then repurpose it everywhere.

I do this in 2 hours and create 10+ pieces of content.

Here's the system..."

The Difference:

  • Before: Impressive but unclear
  • After: Clear and actionable

The APAG Framework

Attention, Perspective, Advantage, and Gamify.

This framework works for:

  • Your newsletter
  • Podcast or YouTube scripts
  • Multiple threads or medium-form posts
  • Sales pages or opt-in pages
  • Course modules

1. Attention — The Art of Hooks and Headlines

Your hook or headline are the most important aspect of your content.

If the hook doesn't catch their attention, are they going to read the rest?

The 3 Things That Make Them Continue Reading:

  • Relevance: How relevant is it to their everyday life?
  • Awareness: Is it simple or complex enough for their level?
  • Effort: How fast will they receive the result?

Building Blocks for the Perfect Hook:

  • The BIG problem
  • The BIG benefit
  • The BIG idea
  • The transformation process
  • Timeframe (use numbers)
  • Negative personal experience

My Best Advice:

Do not write your hook or headline until you have written the entire post. Then you can pull the best aspects of it and craft an attention-grabbing hook.

2. Perspective — Paint a Picture of the Enemy or Why a Perspective Is Wrong

This is where you relate to and amplify the problems that the reader is currently experiencing.

The best way to do this:

Paint a picture of a common perspective on the topic you're writing about.

Place a heavy emphasis on the problems associated with that perspective and the pains they cause.

For more firepower, relate to them with a personal experience you've had with that perspective.

For Perspective, you can use:

  • Problems
  • Benefits
  • Examples
  • Metaphors
  • Quotes or Tweets
  • Personal or popular stories
  • Comparisons
  • Concepts

Your goal is to help them understand. You don't need to solve everything yet.

3. Advantage — Paint a Picture of the Hero, Vision, or Why Your Perspective Is Right

Now that you've painted a picture of their faulty perspective, you can start to shift their perspective to view things the way you do.

This is where you can present:

  • Novel ideas
  • Concepts
  • Social proof
  • Experiences that make your argument credible

Insight: Focus On Education

How can you educate people to the point of understanding your perspective?

What do they need to know in order to understand where you are now?

4. Gamify — Create a Hierarchy of Challenging Goals

When you think of a game, you think of a set hierarchy of goals that give people clarity on what to do.

You "gamify" by giving step-by-step advice on how to achieve the transformation you've described.

You are wrapping everything up into a clear, concise, and actionable way of getting a specific result.

What are the steps that will help them overcome the problem you mentioned in "Perspective?"

What can they implement right now that will get them results?

Insight: Focus On Clarity

What are the exact steps someone can take to reach the desired outcome?

The PPP Framework (For Shorter Content)

Pull, Perspective, Punchline

Pull them in with something that catches attention (like a number or percentage).

Give a unique perspective that came from personal experience and critical thought.

Wrap it all up in a way that makes sense.

Example:

Pull: "Stop posting 10 times a day."

Perspective: "Most creators think more = better. They're wrong."

Punchline: "3 strategic posts > 10 random posts."

This framework works for:

  • Tweets
  • Sections of your newsletter
  • The writing on your landing page
  • Every tweet in a thread

Your job is to capture attention, hold attention, and deliver value to that attention throughout all of your writing.

The Cornerstone Newsletter

All of your writing starts with your newsletter.

You can think of this as a creative practice, as creativity is all about:

  • Having an abundance of ideas, information, and resources to pull from
  • Getting those out on paper (so they are out of your head)
  • Treating them as building blocks for your writing
  • "Connecting the dots" by writing your newsletter

Why do quotes hit so hard?

Because they have depth behind them. A quote is like packaging up 10 years of experience in one sentence.

THIS is why your newsletter is more important than your tweets, threads, or other social posts—even if less people are seeing them.

Writing your newsletter will be the creative highlight of your entire week.

The Outline (The Most Important Part)

You don't KNOW what the newsletter topic will be until you are done with your outline.

You need ample creative firepower, brainstorming, and building blocks to craft a newsletter that makes sense. THEN you come up with a headline that summarizes everything in an impactful way.

Where to Start:

With a topic that you want to write about:

  • A tweet or post that stuck out to you
  • A tweet of yours that did well
  • A high-performing tweet from one of your mentors
  • Your favorite quote
  • A concept, theory, metaphor, or framework

Once you choose an idea to start with, you will plug it into the newsletter outline and it will start to take an entirely new form.

The topic you choose is only the starting point. You will be making that idea your own.

How to Write Each Section:

1. The Introduction

How do I keep my newsletters both attention-grabbing and unique?

By nailing the introduction.

Options for Introductions:

Option 1: Personal experiences or anecdotes

80% of my newsletters start with something like:

"I've always been obsessed with [the topic at hand]."

"I've always despised the modern work culture."

"I've failed at 7 business models."

People value your personal experience. It is what they can relate the most with.

Option 2: A story that illustrates the problem

Was there a story you found in a book, podcast, or from your research online that you can turn into your own?

The mind makes sense of the world via stories. Stories almost always start by hinting at a problem.

Option 3: A common piece of advice that we will disprove

Is there a common perspective or piece of advice that you hear all the time but have a different view on?

If you start your writing with this, the rest of your letter will be focused on backing up your argument and reasoning.

Option 4: A big idea that we will break down

Statistics and other big ideas catch attention well.

"Humans can process 10-50 bits of information per second."

It's interesting and makes people wonder "what's next."

2. Context and Credibility

After you've set the scene by painting a picture of the problem, you will need to give context to the argument, advice, or ideas you are going to share.

My favorite ways to do this:

  1. Find a quote that helps me explain my perspective on the problem
  2. Brainstorm a concept or metaphor that presents a novel perspective
  3. Assume that I know nothing and educate people on the topic from a beginner's perspective

3. A Repurposable List or 1 Piece of Good Advice

Now that we've hooked readers in and gave context, now it's time to deliver on that value.

The best, evergreen way of doing this? A list (in most cases).

4 Ways to Structure the Actionable Section:

  1. A step-by-step system
  2. A framework or concept from your steps
  3. A relevant list
  4. One solution (going all in on that idea)

PART 3: PLATFORM-SPECIFIC STRATEGIES

X (Twitter): The Hook-Driven Platform

X is about hooks.

Your first line determines if someone reads the rest.

Hook Formulas That Work:

  1. Pattern Interrupt:

    • "Stop doing X. Start doing Y."
    • "Most people think X. They're wrong."
    • "The #1 mistake [audience] makes..."

  2. Contrarian Take:

    • "Everyone says X. Here's why that's backwards."
    • "The opposite of what you think is true."

  3. Specific Number/Result:

    • "I grew from 2K to 10K followers doing this..."
    • "3 frameworks that changed everything..."

  4. Question Hook:

    • "Why do most creators fail at [thing]?"
    • "What if you could [desired outcome]?"

The Structure:

  • Line 1: Hook (make them stop scrolling)
  • Lines 2-4: Value (deliver insight quickly)
  • Line 5: Question/CTA (invite engagement)

Tweet Structures That Work:

1. Commentary On Objections Or Advice

These tweets come from paying attention to your inner dialogue.

When you hear something—how do you respond in your head?

2. "Extreme" Advice

You don't have to curse. You just have to transfer the energy by using better words.

What advice worked for you in the past that you want to give to others?

3. Metaphors

Can you help people understand your perspective by using metaphors?

4. Personal Definitions

How would you define a word or phrase?

"Long walks are..."

"Confidence is..."

"Focus is..."

5. Repetitive Advice & Comparisons

Start with a one-sentence piece of advice and brainstorm different variations of that sentence.

6. This Comes After This

Like a story, one thing comes after the next. Write down one word, what it leads to, and what comes next.

7. If This, Then That

"If you [this]..." then what?

8. Harsh Truth With A Hook

Harsh truths in general always work well.

You can add a hook to the top that describes or warns about the advice you are going to give.

9. Your Personal Way Of Doing Something Common

How do you like doing something that other people do?

This requires you to be observant about how you go about your days.

10. Polarity & Repetition

Polarity is about comparing two different things. Either opposites or just making slight, nuanced connections.

11. Unique Advice Or Hacks

These can either be hacks you find online or discover yourself.

12. A List That Describes A Certain Group Of People

Can you describe the traits of yourself or the community you are in?

People will try to lump themselves into having (or not having) those traits.

13. Interesting Observation

Simple. Notice something interesting and talk about it.

14. Subtweeting Yourself

If you are slacking, what advice would you give yourself?

Think of Twitter like a journal where you give high-engaging advice to yourself (but positioned at others).

15. 2-3 Step Punchy Advice

A shorter version of a list that gives a solution to a problem.

16. You Can Do This… But You Have To

What is the desired outcome and what is required to get there?

17. Making People Aware Of A Problem Or Solution

What is causing a problem and what is the solution?

18. Unique Perspectives On A Common Problem

Take a common problem or perspective and give inspiration or a solution to it.

19. The Best & Worst

What is the best, greatest, worst, most terrible thing someone can do?

This is about thinking in extremes, which is entertaining.

PART 4: ATTENTION & ENGAGEMENT PSYCHOLOGY

Dan's 10 Koemmandments of Capturing Attention

People don't remember what you say, they remember how you make them feel.

Humans are emotional beings, not logical.

99% of the population have not developed themselves to the point of being free from—or in control of—their ego.

That is the foundation of capturing and holding attention.

Poking your reader's ego.

Feeding it = making them feel "good."

Starving it = making them feel "bad."

1. Specific Numbers

Using numbers in your hooks, headlines, or tweets will make people stop to see what the numbers relate to.

These can be in the form of:

  • Statistics
  • Dollar amounts
  • Metrics
  • Lists

The more specific the number is, the more it catches people's attention.

2. Pattern Interrupts

A pattern interrupt is something that breaks people out of their normal conditioned patterns.

If someone is scrolling on Twitter and used to a lot of political commentary, a well formatted list tweet will make them stop their scroll.

3. Negativity Bias

The human brain is wired to remember and pay attention to negativity.

This does not mean you have to be negative all the time.

It means you need to use the negative forms of words.

"You are going to achieve great things."

Compared to:

"You will never hit rock bottom again."

The second is most potent. We remember negativity.

4. Group Callout

This is simple, call out the specific person you are talking to.

"If you are in your 20's…"

"Calling all creators, coaches, and freelancers!"

"Fathers are a gift to humankind…"

Even if your audience doesn't fall into the specific group you are calling out, it will allow them to "pick a side" and compare themselves to what you are saying.

5. Problem Callout

Calling out the pains or problems people are experiencing will also make them relate to the post.

MOST people have encountered the same problem or pain as everyone else throughout their life.

If you can describe the feeling accurately, you have attention at your fingertips.

6. Potential Benefit

The opposite of calling out a problem.

Again, pains and benefits. Your mind needs to think in pains and benefits.

7. Social Proof

When you flex your results or credentials, people automatically assume that you know more than them.

They will take your content more seriously and read to find the information they are "missing."

This works 100x better when it isn't viewed as a "flex."

8. Confidence & Conviction

This is by far the most important point here.

You can create highly impactful tweets with confidence alone—it allows other aspects of attention-grabbing to fall into place.

You are right about everything at this very point in time in relation to your goals, vision, beliefs, values, and everything else that forms your perspective of reality.

Your job is to have confidence and conviction in your beliefs and a credible or clear argument to back them up.

Tools you can use to sound more confident:

  • Eliminate words that imply uncertainty
  • Speak in absolutes when possible
  • Exaggerate your point to add energy

Rather than saying:

"It may be wise if some people developed their skillset."

Say:

"It is crucial that everyone on planet earth develops their skillset."

9. Active Voice

Active voice implies that there is a story. It eases people into what you are saying.

Passive voice is often boring and gives away the "mini-story" too early.

10. Warnings & Cautionary Advice

What should people look out for when they are trying to achieve something?

Can you warn them of something they will encounter as they are trying to reach where you are now?

What Holds Attention?

Once the curiosity loop is opened, the mind feels committed to figuring out the rest of the story.

That is what holds attention—crafting a compelling story.

Stories are layers of metaphors, concepts, perspectives, and experiences that take people on a journey.

1. Structure

We aren't in high-school anymore. Long, professional paragraphs don't matter and reduce readability.

How do you improve the structure of all of your content?

  • Bullet lists — use numbers, bullets, and other tactics that help you break apart what you are saying
  • Line breaks — use line breaks whenever possible to emphasize specific sentences
  • Starting short and concise — Use shorter sentences or paragraphs at the start
  • Break apart sentences — use parenthesis and "—" dashes over commas

2. Novel Perspectives

This requires you to get the bulk of your ideas from books, podcasts, or other longer-form content that gives more nuance to certain ideas.

Novelty increases dopamine levels in the brain.

Metaphors, personal experiences, and creating new concepts is how you achieve this.

What Creates Engagement?

Remember: People don't remember what you say, they remember how you make them feel.

1. The feelings that come from education

Learning something new or gaining clarity on some aspect of their life raises dopamine levels in the brain.

You create clarity through novel perspectives, metaphors, stories, personal experiences, and other parts of your life that will help others understand what you are saying.

2. The feelings that come from inspiration

Inspiration is different from motivation.

You are helping people connect the dots on their own, giving them the clarity to make their own decisions and change their behavior.

People feel good when they think they came up with the answer themselves—but will still attribute that feeling to you.

3. The feeling that comes from entertainment

Can you throw in a joke, meme, or interesting point of view?

Entertainment, education, and inspiration are the main goals of your content.

PART 5: INFLUENCE, PERSUASION, & STORYTELLING

The Building Blocks of Persuasion

Every. Single. Skill you can learn that will help you succeed in this space is amplified by persuasion.

Persuasion is what separates the accounts that grow from those that don't.

Persuasion is what separates the accounts that make money from those that don't.

Persuasion, in a nutshell, is how you convince someone to believe something.

I like to think of it as creating an argument to adopt your perspective and see the benefit in viewing things in the way that you do.

The 3 Questions to Keep in Mind:

  1. What are the downsides of viewing things from a certain perspective and the day-to-day pains associated with it?
  2. What are the upsides of viewing things from a new perspective and the day-to-day benefits associated with it?
  3. What is the process for getting there and can you make it simple enough to act on immediately?

Our goal with persuasion comes down to 3 things:

  1. Sparking positive behavior change — by working people through their levels of awareness
  2. Getting the conversion — Making the sale, scheduling a date, getting engagement, having your content shared
  3. Mutual benefit — the difference between manipulation and persuasion is the benefit. We want to aim to deliver more value than what we receive.

The 6 Building Blocks of Persuasion (From Robert Cialdini's Influence):

1. Reciprocity

Our social evolution makes us feel compelled to return favors.

When you give value without expectation of repayment, others feel compelled to return the favor.

This can take form as a free guide, a bonus product, sending someone a strategy, or just wishing someone a happy Monday.

2. Commitment / Consistency

Humans feel obligated to follow through with their word.

When they say they are going to do something, they begin to justify to themselves why they should do it.

A "commitment" here can be as simple as liking a post, clicking a link, filling out a form, etc.

3. Social Proof

People are not confident in their day to day actions. We are constantly looking to confirm our beliefs, choices, and actions.

Social proof—like testimonials, results, and word of mouth marketing is the most powerful persuasion tool in your arsenal.

4. Liking

You are more likely to trust and be persuaded by somebody that you like.

This displays the power of a properly executed personal brand.

With a personal brand, you are guiding people's perception of you.

5. Authority

We tend to trust authoritative figures much quicker than less authoritative people.

Authority is determined by:

  • Level of expertise
  • Accomplishments in a specific area
  • Well known labels, symbols, or achievements
  • Amount of social proof
  • Speaking with confidence and using absolutes (wisely)

6. Scarcity

In other words, FOMO.

Scarcity is a gauge for how valuable something is—simple supply and demand.

This can be worked into your content or products.

Strategic Engagement vs. Random Engagement

Most people engage randomly.

Tier 2: Growing Accounts (10 comments/day)

  • Build connections

Tier 3: Emerging Accounts (5 comments/day)

  • Accounts with 1K-10K followers
  • Supportive engagement
  • Mentor mindset
  • Build community

The Comment Formula:

  1. Acknowledge:

    • Show you read their post
    • Reference specific point

  2. Add Value:

    • Share your insight
    • Add example or framework
    • Show expertise

  3. Invite Connection:

    • Ask thoughtful question
    • Invite conversation
    • Build relationship

This is exactly right.

Those who lead with client problems first see 3x faster growth.

Have you found this messaging shift to be the biggest breakthrough for your clients?

Why This Works:

  • Shows you read the post
  • Adds your value
  • Invites conversation
  • Builds relationship

Engagement Time:

  • Better than 100 random comments
  • Real relationships vs. spam

Non-Needy Networking

Networking is how you get more eyes on your writing.

DMs are how you network.

Most people suck at DMs.

Never, and I repeat never ever ever expect a response if all you send is:

  • Hi or hey
  • How are you
  • Can you follow me back
  • A straight sales pitch with no prior connection
  • A long block of text that takes too much time to read

7 Steps to Non-Needy Networking:

1. Find somebody that you WANT to DM

Reach out to people that:

  • You are inspired by
  • You would want to work with
  • You would want to strategize with
  • You see potential for mutual benefit

2. Send them an inspired compliment

Find a piece of their content, work, or current projects that you are truly inspired by. Send it to them and tell them how it resonated with you.

3. Show interest in them

Ask them about:

  • Their goals
  • What they are building
  • What they do for work

4. Lead with value

Your first options are to:

  • See where you can help
  • Send actionable tips
  • Send resources, systems, or videos

Don't go straight for a transaction when you haven't given anything first.

5. Get on a call to make a deeper connection

This part is optional but recommended.

Making that "face-to-face" interaction is priceless.

6. Follow up with value

Remember their goals and keep an eye out for any content, resources, or people you can send their way.

7. Follow up with an ask

By this point you've built a pretty damn solid connection. You've given value to the point where they are ready to return the favor.

From here you can:

  • Ask them to join a mastermind group
  • Send them one of your posts
  • Ask any specific questions you have

The Evergreen Content Style: Lists

People. Love. Lists.

Lists lists lists.

They just work every single time. Especially for those just starting out.

Lists have to be the one style of content that will never go out of style.

Why Do Lists Work So Well?

  1. Every bullet point gives the reader a reason to engage

If the reader agrees with any of those points, they should like, comment, or share.

  1. They catch attention

Lists and numbers make people stop and read.

  1. They can be repurposed over and over

  4. Format it into a carousel on Instagram

PART 8: CREATIVITY VS PRODUCTIVITY

The Balance

Digital writing is a balance of creativity and productivity.

Becoming a writer demands a restful, yet disciplined, lifestyle.

There's a reason ancient masters, spiritual teachers, legendary advertisers, and the world's top filmmakers seem to "do nothing" all day.

It's because your brain can't be creative in the constant state of narrow focus that the western world has been conditioned into.

The 2 Networks in Our Brain:

The Default Mode Network (for creativity):

When the Default Mode Network is active in your brain, your subconscious solves complex and creative problems.

You activate the default mode network when you are not focused on an external task.

This can happen when your focus shifts inward:

  • Reading or listening to things that make you think big picture
  • Intense exercise
  • Walking in nature
  • Your favorite music, meditation, or laying out in the sun

The Task Positive Network (for productivity):

Deep work is best done in a structured and organized fashion.

When your creative work aligns with the goals and tasks you have planned for a specific work block, you meet the requirements for reaching the flow state.

The Process:

  1. Having an idea
  2. Dissecting it and brainstorming along with it
  3. Letting it sit while you rest and the DMN activates
  4. Like magic, the "aha!" moment of excitement and passion will hit you

Insight: Brainstorm & Let It Simmer

When we outline our first piece of writing, you will do it a week in advance.

As you go about your week, you will be writing the post spaced out during the week.

This gives you time to rest, consume related information, and come up with a unique perspective that will separate you from other writers online.

Insight: Bringing Order To Chaos

Productive work blocks are best used to organize and edit the mess of a brainstorm you create during your creative sessions.

Trying to write without ample ideas and a plan is a road to nowhere.

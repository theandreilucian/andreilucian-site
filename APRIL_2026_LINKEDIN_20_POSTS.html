Post 1 · Wed Apr 1 · Education

Your profile isn’t a bio — it’s a risk screen

Hiring managers and clients don’t “discover your soul” in 40 seconds.

They answer one question:

Is this person credible enough for another minute?

If your profile looks like this, you’re asking for trust without evidence:

  • Headline = pile of titles
  • About = vague inspiration
  • Featured = empty (but you say you “create content”)

Pass the 10-second test. A stranger should see:

  • Who you help
  • What problem you work on
  • Why you’re not guessing — proof (experience, results, method, POV)

Headline = positioning — not cleverness.

About = who you help, what they get, how work with you runs — no buzzword soup.

Posting = ship at ~80%. Refine from feedback.

Perfectionism is a tax on visibility.

Post 6 · Tue Apr 7 · Education

The “first line” test (writing online)

Before you hit publish, ask:

If I delete the first line… is there still a point?

If the answer is no — you don’t have a post.

You have a mood.

Your first line is a contract:

“I’m about to use your attention for something worth it.”

Then deliver 3 things:

  • A claim — what you believe
  • A reason — experience or logic
  • A takeaway — what to do next

The internet isn’t starving for content.

It’s starving for clear thinking that respects the scroll.

Early on in my career, I thought it would be more profitable if I just stacked my workload instead of delegating.

What a mistake that was.

I was caught up in very LOW ROI tasks and obligations that I could have easily given to an entry-level employee. I was doing work that anyone could do. I was optimizing for busyness, not for impact.

I thought I was being smart by doing everything myself. I thought I was saving money. I thought I was being efficient.

I was wrong.

Now in hindsight, the founder/CEO needs to be the visionary.

Some of the most important tasks that a CEO does are actually nothing. Just thinking.

THE CEO ROLE FRAMEWORK:

1. VISION (Not execution)
   • Set direction, not tasks
   • Think strategically, not tactically
   • Focus on what, not how
   • Your job is to see the future

2. DELEGATION (Not doing)
   • Give low ROI tasks to entry-level employees
   • Free yourself for high ROI work
   • Trust your team
   • Build systems, not dependencies

3. THINKING TIME (Essential)
   • Schedule time to think
   • No meetings, no tasks, just thinking
   • Strategic decisions require space
   • Your best work happens in silence

4. HIGH ROI ONLY (The rule)
   • Only do what only you can do
   • Delegate everything else
   • Your time is your most valuable asset
   • Protect it ruthlessly

I learned this the hard way. I was doing $15/hour work when I should have been doing $1,500/hour work. I was optimizing for activity, not impact.

The CEO's job is to think. To see opportunities. To make strategic decisions. Not to do entry-level tasks.

Delegate the work. Keep the thinking.

What low ROI tasks are you still doing?

I was wrong.

Pay yourself upwards of 50%, and keep 50% in the business.

YOU HAVE TO HAVE MONEY IN THE BUSINESS TO GROW.

THE 50/50 RULE FRAMEWORK:

1. PAY YOURSELF (50%)
   • Take fair compensation
   • Don't drain everything
   • Leave room for growth
   • Balance personal and business needs

2. KEEP IN BUSINESS (50%)
   • Cash for opportunities
   • Cash for emergencies
   • Cash for growth
   • Cash for investments

3. GROWTH REQUIRES CAPITAL
   • You can't grow without cash
   • Opportunities require money
   • Scaling needs capital
   • Empty accounts don't scale

4. THE MISTAKE
   • Draining accounts = no growth
   • $0 balance = no opportunities
   • Max salary = max problems
   • Short-term thinking, long-term cost

I learned this the hard way. I had opportunities I couldn't take because I had no cash. I had to pass on deals because I drained everything. I limited my growth by being greedy.

The business needs money to grow. You need money to scale. Opportunities require capital.

Keep money in the business. It's not sitting there doing nothing. It's waiting for opportunities. It's fuel for growth.

I've built teams across 3 countries.

US warehouses. Pakistani core team. Philippines admin.

Here's the framework I use for hiring:

THE HIRING FRAMEWORK:

1. DEFINE THE ROLE (Before you post)
   • What problem are you solving?
   • What outcome do you need?
   • What skills are non-negotiable?
   • What can be taught vs. must-have?

2. TEST BEFORE YOU HIRE
   • Give them a real project
   • See how they think, not just what they say
   • Watch their process, not just results
   • Test for ownership, not just skills

3. HIRE FOR ATTITUDE, TRAIN FOR SKILL
   • Skills can be taught
   • Ownership can't
   • Discipline can't
   • Self-education can't

4. START SMALL, SCALE FAST
   • Hire one person, prove the role
   • Document the system
   • Then hire more
   • Don't hire 10 people before proving 1 works

Most entrepreneurs hire too fast, fire too slow, and don't document systems.

I've made every mistake. Hired the wrong people. Kept them too long. Didn't document processes. Paid the price.

Now I hire differently:

I test first. I hire for ownership. I document everything. I start small and scale fast.

The result? Teams that run without me. Systems that scale. Companies that grow.

Your team is your biggest asset or your biggest liability.

Hire for discipline, ownership, and self-education. Train for skills.

   • Eat for performance
   • Avoid energy crashes
   • Fuel your body
   • Nutrition is energy

   • Daily movement
   • Strength training
   • Cardio for endurance
   • Exercise creates energy

4. MANAGE STRESS (Critical)
   • Reduce stress
   • Manage pressure
   • Optimize recovery
   • Stress drains energy

Most people don't manage energy. They burn out. They crash. They can't perform.

I optimize sleep. I optimize nutrition. I optimize exercise. I manage stress.

The result? High energy. Consistent performance. No crashes.

Energy management is performance optimization. Optimize it.

Entrepreneurship is about taking risks.

But smart risks. Calculated risks. Not stupid risks.

I learned this losing $75,000 on a bad investment. I listened to wrong people. I didn't do my research.

Here's the framework:

THE RISK MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK:

1. ASSESS RISKS (First)
   • What are the risks?
   • What's the downside?
   • What could go wrong?
   • Know what you're risking

2. EVALUATE REWARDS (Second)
   • What's the upside?
   • What's the potential?
   • What could go right?
   • Know what you're gaining

3. CALCULATE ODDS (Third)
   • What are the odds?
   • What's the probability?
   • Is it worth it?
   • Math over feelings

4. TAKE CALCULATED RISKS (Finally)
   • Take smart risks
   • Avoid stupid risks
   • Calculated, not reckless
   • Risk management is key

Most entrepreneurs take stupid risks. They don't assess. They don't calculate. They gamble.

I assess risks. I evaluate rewards. I calculate odds. I take calculated risks. I vet my advisors.

The result? Smart decisions. Better outcomes. Managed risk.

Risk is part of entrepreneurship. Manage it smartly.

   • Build for tomorrow
   • Short-term pain, long-term gain

3. BUILD SYSTEMS (Not goals)
   • Systems compound
   • Goals don't
   • Build for long-term
   • Systems last

4. STAY DISCIPLINED (Consistently)
   • Discipline compounds
   • Consistency wins
   • Stay the course
   • Discipline is everything

Most people think short-term. They want quick wins. They don't build for long-term.

I think in decades. I sacrifice short-term. I build systems. I stay disciplined. I keep money in the business for growth.

The result? Long-term success. Compounding results. Real wealth.

Long-term thinking beats short-term thinking. Every time.

What are you building for the long-term?

I deleted the thing nobody clapped for

Then I did it again.

Hey,

First year creating in public, I shipped something I was proud of.

Waited an hour. Almost no response. One person I knew said "nice."

Draft → shipped → 4 DMs

I deleted it.

Did that for months. Create. Flop. Delete. Rewrite. Flop. Delete. I told myself I was "curating." Truth? I was training myself to quit the second the world didn't clap.

The problem wasn't the work. It was my attachment to instant validation.

Now I keep the flops. I study them. What was weak? What was unclear? What would I do differently? I ship the next day anyway.

Low response isn't a verdict on your worth. It's feedback on the packaging — or timing — or both.

When nothing moved for weeks, I almost stopped creating entirely. Thought maybe I wasn't built for this. Half the builders I know had the same night. The difference is who came back the next morning.

Month 1–2 I made $0. Still created. Month 3: $223. Proof that output compounds before confidence does.

Took me a year to stop taking silence personally. Now it's feedback. What was weak? What was unclear? What do I try next?

Deletion feels good for five minutes. Learning compounds forever.

Creating in public is a muscle. Every time you delete because nobody clapped, you atrophy. Every time you ship anyway, you get stronger.

If you deleted something recently because it "flopped" — you're human. Stop deleting. Start studying.

The piece that changes your path is probably the one after the one you're embarrassed by.

Keep creating.

The post I deleted was about a client win I was proud of. I softened the proof because I didn't want to look arrogant. Then nobody responded and I blamed the topic. Maybe I should have blamed the softening.

Deletion taught my brain: silence = shame. Ship again = risk shame again. Easier to delete. Easier to pretend it never happened.

That loop almost ended my creating phase entirely.

The night I decided to stop deleting, I re-posted a version of the same idea with clearer proof and a simpler hook. Not viral. Better than silence. Three replies. One DM. Small. Real.

That's when I learned: low response is data, not identity.

Now I keep a flop folder. Sunday nights. What was the hook? Too long? Too vague? Wrong time? No proof line? I write one sentence: "Next time I will ___." Then I close the laptop.

Creating in public without studying flops is just repeating mistakes with confidence.

I think about friends who stopped posting after one bad week. Not because they lacked skill — because deletion trained them to quit at the first quiet signal.

Your next post isn't judged by the last one's likes. It's judged by whether you show up again.

If you deleted something this month — write it again. Clearer hook. One proof line. Ship it. Keep it. Study it Sunday.

The piece that changes your path is probably the one after the one you're embarrassed by.

I share these stories because Monday's tactics only work if you survive the chapters nobody posts about.

Month two silence. Deleted posts. $0 months. The first invoice that scared me. Boring Tuesdays. Sunday nights with honest eyes. That's the real personal brand journey — not the highlight reel.

Almost three years later I have 5.3K on 𝕏, 16.2K on LinkedIn, 1,100+ subscribers, and clients who found me in public. Same person who almost quit in the void. Different stamina.

Your details will differ. The lesson won't: keep building when it's boring. Protect your health. Ship when it's ugly. Study when it flops. Stay in the arena.

Personal branding online isn't a performance for strangers. It's the accumulated evidence that you kept going when quitting was rational.

I still have doubt days. I still open my work and think it's not good enough. I ship anyway. That's the life lesson behind every tactic I teach on Monday.

Don't quit ten percent before the bend. Your future self is counting on today's rep — even when today's rep feels invisible.

Thank you for reading.

– Andrei

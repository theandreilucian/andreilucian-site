I tracked every input for 30 days — here's the spreadsheet

Posts, replies, DMs. Not vibes.

Last month I stopped guessing and started counting. Thirty days. Three numbers daily: posts shipped, replies sent, DMs sent. Followers optional.

Why? Because I'd been mood-measuring growth — good day if the timeline moved, bad day if it didn't. That's casino thinking.

The rules

Non-negotiable floor: 3 posts, 30 replies, 3 DMs. Stretch goal: 50 replies on focus days. No editing the spreadsheet to feel better.

Week 1 avg: 3.0 posts · 41 replies · 8 DMs · follower delta: +22
Week 2 avg: 3.0 posts · 48 replies · 11 DMs · follower delta: +31
Week 3 avg: 2.8 posts · 52 replies · 14 DMs · follower delta: +44
Week 4 avg: 3.0 posts · 47 replies · 9 DMs · follower delta: +38

Nothing viral. No hero post. The graph bent because inputs were boring and consistent.

What surprised me

Week 4 — +38 followers, zero viral posts

Replies correlated with profile visits more than my own posts did. Week 3 had my worst post of the month — and my best DM week. Second-order thinking: the room matters more than the stage when you're under 2K.

Track inputs long enough and anxiety gets replaced by math.

Day 11 I tried to cheat — counted a retweet as engagement. Deleted it from the sheet. The point is honesty with the numbers, not vanity with the spreadsheet.

By day 30 I stopped opening the follower tab first. Inputs became the scoreboard. Anxiety dropped. Shipping got easier.

You don't need a fancy tool. Notes app or Google Sheet. Four columns. Thirty rows. That's the whole experiment.

Steal the sheet

  • Four columns: date · posts · replies · DMs
  • One row per day — 30 rows minimum
  • Review Fridays — one adjustment, not five
  • Ban follower checks except Sunday night

Month 3: $223 arrived the month after I started treating growth like accounting. Coincidence? Maybe. I'm not going back to vibes.

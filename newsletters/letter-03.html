The 7 months nobody was watching (and why I'm glad)

Flat graph · loud doubt · quiet reps

There's a stretch every builder has where the graph lies. You show up. Nothing moves. You wonder if the platform is broken or you are.

Mine was seven months. Not a weekend of discouragement — seven months of posting into silence.

Month 1–2: $0. Nobody DMs back. I rewrite my bio twice. Still $0.
Month 3: First real reply chain. Not viral — one conversation. I remember the username.
Month 4: I commit to 50 replies on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Awkward. Works.
Month 5: 3 posts/day becomes automatic. Still under 800 followers.
Month 6: Someone quotes my post. Small. I screenshot it like an idiot. Worth it.
Month 7: Graph bends. Not spikes — bend. DMs pick up. Month 3 revenue: $223.

If I'd quit at month 4 — the most dangerous month — you'd never read this. Most people quit in month 4.

What the flat months built

Almost quit at month 6

A voice that wasn't borrowed. Thick skin. A reply style. A folder of flops I could learn from without an audience judging every miss.

Invisibility isn't punishment. It's rehearsal without stakes.

5.3K on 𝕏 didn't feel like winning when it happened. It felt like the graph finally telling the truth about work I'd already done.

If you're in the flat

The flat months taught me reply craft — how to ask one good question, how to reference a line from someone's post without sounding like a bot, how to show up in the same rooms until faces recognize your name.

If you're in month 3–5 right now, you're not failing. You're in the tutorial. The game is designed to feel like rejection before it feels like traction.

  • Count inputs, not followers — 30 days minimum
  • Pick 5 accounts — show up in their replies daily
  • Write one "month X still here" post — document, don't perform
  • Set a review date 90 days out — not a quit date

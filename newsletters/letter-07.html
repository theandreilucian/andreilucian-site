The autopsy — why 11 likes beat 1,100

Posted anyway. 11 likes. Small by viral standards. Large by my standards at the time. Four DMs. One from someone at 8K who said they'd been watching. One from a future client. One from a guy who became a regular in my replies. One that went nowhere — still counts.

No viral spike. No follower avalanche. A small proof that honesty travels differently than tips. Retroactively, it worked because: specific numbers ($0, month 2), no advice — just state, vulnerable without begging, short — mobile-length.

Ready is a feeling. Shipped is a fact.

Dan Koe's best work comes from one decision made early: write from a point of view, ship before it's perfect. My version: publish before you're ready or stay invisible forever. Almost three years of daily reps is just this one decision, repeated. Not bravery. Repetition.

Indecision feels safe. It's the most expensive habit in the creator economy. The single decision is ship before ready — but the practice is smaller: ship one imperfect post today. One reply you're not sure about. One DM that might get ignored.

Close the notes app. Open the compose box. That's the decision. Track DMs for forty-eight hours after shipping — not likes. The posts you almost delete are often the ones that start the conversations that change your trajectory.

The DM that became a client

One of the four DMs from the 11-like post: "This sounds like where I am." No pitch from me. Three messages back and forth. Two weeks later they asked about ghostwriting. Not because I sold — because the post sounded like a person they'd trust with an embarrassing month.

That's the ROI of witness writing at low follower counts. You don't need reach. You need one right person to feel seen.

Why likes lie

Likes are applause. DMs are conversations. Conversations become relationships. Relationships become revenue — months later, on a lag, when trust deposits enough to withdraw.

If you're under 2K, run a 48-hour DM count after every vulnerable post. You'll learn faster than any analytics tool.

Almost three years later

I still ship posts I'm scared of. The difference now: I trust the autopsy. Specific numbers. No advice. Short. The format survived because the decision survived — not because the fear disappeared.

Your notes app is a graveyard of almosts. Pick one draft from the graveyard this week. Ship it with one number added. Count what happens in DMs — not likes.

Vulnerability without begging

The 11-like post worked because it was vulnerable without begging. No "like if you agree." No "follow for more." Just state: month 2, $0, still here. That's witness writing — the reader fills in their own story.

Begging shrinks you. Witness expands the reader. They see themselves in your numbers. That's when DMs start — not when you ask, when they recognize.

Find the post that embarrasses you and ask: is it begging or witnessing? Edit until it's witness.

How to engineer a shipping habit

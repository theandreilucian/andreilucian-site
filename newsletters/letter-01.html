Your posts sound like everyone else's (and the algorithm knows)

Generic content dies in the feed. Here's how I fixed mine.

Read your last 5 posts out loud. If they could've been written by any account in your niche — you don't have a content problem. You have a you problem.

I know because I was that account. Month 1: $0. Month 2: $0. Tweeting growth tips I hadn't earned yet. Copy-paste energy. 2 likes from bots.

Almost three years later: 5.3K on 𝕏 organic. 16.2K on LinkedIn. 1,100+ newsletter subs. The content changed because the source changed — I stopped summarizing the internet and started reporting from my own lab.

The swap that mattered

Generic tips sink · your proof floats

Before: "5 tips to grow on 𝕏." After: "I ran 50 replies a day for 7 days. Day 3 someone DM'd me. Here's the log."

Same platform. Same goal. One sounds like a search result. One sounds like a person.

People don't share information anymore. They share evidence.

What only you can write

Your failed months. Your awkward DMs. The post that got 11 likes but started a friendship. The Sunday review where you killed a hook that felt cringe.

AI can write tips. It can't write Month 2 at $0 and still showing up.

The receipt test (run on your next draft)
  1. Does it include one number you measured?
  2. Does it include one thing that didn't work?
  3. Could 10,000 accounts post this unchanged?
  4. If yes to the last one — rewrite or delete.

The algorithm doesn't hate you. It just can't rank what it can't distinguish. When 10,000 accounts post the same thread structure with the same advice, the feed treats you like interchangeable inventory.

My fix wasn't a new hook formula. It was a new rule: if I hadn't lived it this week, I didn't post it. Boring constraint. Massive filter.

Start with one post that's uncomfortably specific. A number you measured. A DM you got. A day you wanted to quit. That's the raw material everything else builds from.

This week

  • Pull your top 3 posts by replies — note what they have in common
  • Write one post only you could write — one fail, one number
  • Cut every sentence that sounds like a course module

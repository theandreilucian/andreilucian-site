The GAP Framework

Every tweet you post should serve one of three purposes:

G - Growth

Purpose: Get more eyes on your content. These tweets are designed to go viral, get shared, and attract new followers.

How: Study what's working for top creators. Take their concepts, add your unique perspective, and make it your own.

Examples: Contrarian takes, frameworks, "hot takes" that make people think, listicles, "things I learned" threads.

Ratio: 60-70% of your content should be growth-focused.

A - Authority

Purpose: Build trust and credibility. Show people you know what you're talking about.

How: Share your real experiences, lessons learned, wins and losses. Be vulnerable. Show your process.

Examples: Case studies, behind-the-scenes, mistakes you made, results you got, frameworks you use.

Ratio: 20-30% of your content should establish authority.

P - Personality

Purpose: Make people remember you. Show who you are beyond the advice.

How: Share your quirks, opinions, daily life, humor, struggles. Be human.

Examples: Personal stories, hot takes on random topics, what you're reading/watching, your process, failures.

Ratio: 10-20% of your content should show personality.

The Balance:

Most creators get this wrong. They either:

  • Post only growth content (looks spammy, no trust)
  • Post only authority content (smart but boring, no reach)
  • Post only personality (fun but no value, no growth)

The magic happens when you balance all three.

Growth gets you seen.

Authority makes you trusted.

Personality makes you remembered.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Posting only growth content: You'll look spammy and won't build trust.
  2. Posting only authority content: You'll be smart but boring, and won't get reach.
  3. Posting only personality: You'll be fun but provide no value, and won't grow.
  4. Engaging like a bot: "Agreed" and "Indeed" don't build relationships.
  5. DMing to pitch: Connect first. Build relationships. Then maybe offer value.
  6. Not replying to comments: If someone takes time to engage, reply. Always.
  7. Giving up too early: Most people quit right before they break through.

Your posts sound like everyone else's (and the algorithm knows)

Generic content dies in the feed. Here's how I fixed mine.

Read your last 5 posts out loud. If they could've been written by any account in your niche — you don't have a content problem. You have a you problem.

I know because I was that account. Month 1: $0. Month 2: $0. Tweeting growth tips I hadn't earned yet. Copy-paste energy. 2 likes from bots.

Almost three years later: 5.3K on 𝕏 organic. 16.2K on LinkedIn. 1,100+ newsletter subs. The content changed because the source changed — I stopped summarizing the internet and started reporting from my own lab.

The swap that mattered

Generic tips sink · your proof floats

Before: "5 tips to grow on 𝕏." After: "I ran 50 replies a day for 7 days. Day 3 someone DM'd me. Here's the log."

Same platform. Same goal. One sounds like a search result. One sounds like a person.

People don't share information anymore. They share evidence.

What only you can write

Your failed months. Your awkward DMs. The post that got 11 likes but started a friendship. The Sunday review where you killed a hook that felt cringe.

AI can write tips. It can't write Month 2 at $0 and still showing up.

The receipt test (run on your next draft)
  1. Does it include one number you measured?
  2. Does it include one thing that didn't work?
  3. Could 10,000 accounts post this unchanged?
  4. If yes to the last one — rewrite or delete.

The algorithm doesn't hate you. It just can't rank what it can't distinguish. When 10,000 accounts post the same thread structure with the same advice, the feed treats you like interchangeable inventory.

My fix wasn't a new hook formula. It was a new rule: if I hadn't lived it this week, I didn't post it. Boring constraint. Massive filter.

Start with one post that's uncomfortably specific. A number you measured. A DM you got. A day you wanted to quit. That's the raw material everything else builds from.

This week

  • Pull your top 3 posts by replies — note what they have in common
  • Write one post only you could write — one fail, one number
  • Cut every sentence that sounds like a course module

I tracked every input for 30 days — here's the spreadsheet

Posts, replies, DMs. Not vibes.

Last month I stopped guessing and started counting. Thirty days. Three numbers daily: posts shipped, replies sent, DMs sent. Followers optional.

Why? Because I'd been mood-measuring growth — good day if the timeline moved, bad day if it didn't. That's casino thinking.

The rules

Non-negotiable floor: 3 posts, 30 replies, 3 DMs. Stretch goal: 50 replies on focus days. No editing the spreadsheet to feel better.

Week 1 avg: 3.0 posts · 41 replies · 8 DMs · follower delta: +22
Week 2 avg: 3.0 posts · 48 replies · 11 DMs · follower delta: +31
Week 3 avg: 2.8 posts · 52 replies · 14 DMs · follower delta: +44
Week 4 avg: 3.0 posts · 47 replies · 9 DMs · follower delta: +38

Nothing viral. No hero post. The graph bent because inputs were boring and consistent.

What surprised me

Week 4 — +38 followers, zero viral posts

Replies correlated with profile visits more than my own posts did. Week 3 had my worst post of the month — and my best DM week. Second-order thinking: the room matters more than the stage when you're under 2K.

Track inputs long enough and anxiety gets replaced by math.

Day 11 I tried to cheat — counted a retweet as engagement. Deleted it from the sheet. The point is honesty with the numbers, not vanity with the spreadsheet.

By day 30 I stopped opening the follower tab first. Inputs became the scoreboard. Anxiety dropped. Shipping got easier.

You don't need a fancy tool. Notes app or Google Sheet. Four columns. Thirty rows. That's the whole experiment.

Steal the sheet

  • Four columns: date · posts · replies · DMs
  • One row per day — 30 rows minimum
  • Review Fridays — one adjustment, not five
  • Ban follower checks except Sunday night

Month 3: $223 arrived the month after I started treating growth like accounting. Coincidence? Maybe. I'm not going back to vibes.

Stop optimizing hooks. Pick one metric for 90 days.

Confused creators chase everything. Clear ones count one thing.

You can't optimize followers, revenue, newsletter subs, and brand deals simultaneously at 400 followers. You'll drive yourself insane and post like a nervous intern.

Pick one primary metric for 90 days. I chose replies + DMs when I was stuck under 2K. Not because a guru said so — because replies were the only signal that meant a human cared.

The three forks

Optimize replies

+ Best under 2K — you learn rooms, voices, what lands

− Slow vanity metric if you ignore posts entirely

Optimize followers

+ Feels motivating short-term

− Chases ghosts — empty growth, no DMs

Optimize revenue

+ Forces real offers early

− Brutal without trust — you'll spam

I ran replies first. Followers followed. Revenue followed that. Order matters.

My 90-day card

Pick replies — ignore follower panic

Written on a note card taped to my monitor: 50 replies on focus days. 20 minimum otherwise. 3 posts. 5 DMs/week. Check followers Sunday only.

That's it. No hook research rabbit holes until the card was green for a month.

Clarity is choosing what to ignore.

Write yours

I see creators change their metric every time a post flops. That's not strategy — that's mood management. Pick one number. Defend it for 90 days. Then reassess with data.

My card lived on my monitor for a year. Laminated it eventually. Stupid? Maybe. Worked? Absolutely.

  • One metric — replies, subs, or revenue
  • One floor number — daily or weekly
  • One ban — what you won't check daily
  • End date — 90 days from today

The machine I run every morning (before coffee, before doubt)

Inputs → lag → output. No inspiration required.

I don't wake up creative. I wake up on a schedule. The machine runs whether I feel like it or not.

6:30–7:15 — Write

Three posts. Not three perfect posts. One story, one lesson, one proof line. Batch when I can. Timer: 45 minutes. Phone in another room.

2:00–2:45 — Reply

Inputs → lag → output → review

Fifty replies on focus days. Thirty minimum otherwise. Not "great post!" — questions, additions, short stories. Replies are mini-posts in someone else's room.

7:30–8:00 — DM

Five messages to people I genuinely want to know. Not pitches. Not "collab?" — specific, human, short.

Sunday — 20 minutes

Winners folder. Flops folder. One adjustment for next week. Follower check happens here — nowhere else.

The algorithm isn't my manager. The calendar is.

5.3K on 𝕏 and 16.2K on LinkedIn are lagging indicators of this loop — not of one brilliant thread.

Build your version
  1. Write window — same time, same duration
  2. Reply window — separate from writing
  3. DM cap — small enough to be human
  4. Review — weekly, timed, boring

Month 1: $0. Month 2: $0. Month 3: $223. Month 13: $7,502. Imagine if I had quit.

LinkedIn runs on a lighter version of the same machine — 3 posts per week, same review, same proof standard. 16.2K there didn't come from a different personality. Different surface, same operator.

Inspiration is optional. The calendar isn't. Build the machine small enough that you can run it on your worst day — then scale the numbers, not the complexity.

The specificity ladder

Climb the specificity ladder

Level 1: "post more" — invisible. Level 2: "post 3x/day" — slightly better. Level 3: "3x/day: story, lesson, proof" — useful. Level 4: numbers + failure + timeline — shareable.

50 replies a day for 7 days — field report

Awkward → pattern → DMs. The log.

I didn't theorize about engagement. I ran 50 replies a day for 7 days and wrote down what happened.

Mon: Forced. 52 replies in 58 min. Felt like homework. 0 DMs.
Tue: Noticed question-replies beat praise. 48 replies. 1 profile visit spike.
Wed: Someone replied back "finally someone who gets it." First real conversation in weeks.
Thu: Copy-paste day — 2 replies got ignored mid-thread. Lesson: read first.
Fri: Shorter replies. More specific. 50 exactly. 2 DMs.
Sat: 30 replies — rest day floor. Still showed up.
Sun: Review. Follower bump small but real. Kept 30 as floor, 50 on focus days.

What failed

Generic praise. Replying without reading. Treating it like a checkbox. People smell automation.

What worked

Replies → visits → DMs → followers

One idea per reply. One question. Referencing a specific line from their post. Showing up in the same 5 rooms daily so faces recognize you.

Under 2K followers, replies are your timeline.

This experiment did more for my graph than a month of timeline-only posting. I went from invisible to 5.3K organic over years — but the bend started in weeks like this.

The awkward phase lasts about 3 days. Then your brain starts pattern-matching which reply styles get responses. By day 7 you're not forcing it — you're hunting.

I kept 30 replies as my floor after the experiment. Fifty on days I have energy. The habit survived the sprint.

Run it yourself

  • Pick 5 accounts — your size or slightly bigger
  • 50 replies/day × 7 days — timer on
  • Log DMs and profile visits, not likes
  • Sunday: one rule change for next week

Why 47 people open every email (and it's not the subject line)

Trust compounds slower than hooks — and lasts longer

I used to obsess over subject lines. A/B tests. Emoji debates. Curiosity gaps. Then I looked at my opens over 6 months.

The same 40–50 people opened almost every time. Not because I mastered copy — because they'd decided I was worth reading.

How trust got built

Not in the newsletter first. On 𝕏 and LinkedIn — months of showing up with the same voice, same proof, same bluntness. The newsletter was the deposit account. Social was the daily transfers.

1,100+ subscribers now. The growth came after the voice was recognizable — not before.

Scroll-stop vs Tuesday-open

80% short finds · 20% long keeps

Short-form win

+ Hooks, speed, testing ideas

− Borrowed attention — gone tomorrow

Long-form win

+ Depth, failure, frameworks

− Slower — needs consistency

Hooks rent attention. Trust owns it.

The ratio I run

Roughly 80% short (𝕏 + LinkedIn), 20% long (newsletter). Short finds people. Long keeps them. Month 13: $7,502 came from people who'd been reading for months — not from one viral hook.

Subject lines matter for the first open. Trust matters for the hundredth. Build for the hundredth.

Reply to every newsletter response for a month. That's how 47 regulars becomes 200. Care is a conversation, not a broadcast.

This week

  • One long piece with a failure + number + framework
  • Three shorts that stand alone — not "newsletter out!"
  • Reply to everyone who engages on the long piece
  • Start at 50 subs if you haven't — practice care early

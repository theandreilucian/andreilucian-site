## Introduction

**The Intelligence Age** is a blog post by **Sam Altman** that declares the overall success of **deep learning** as a path to **AGI**. It underscores the ability of **scaling laws** to predict when large language models will reach a level of performance that meets some definition of AGI. Below are three key quotes and a short reader’s take.

---

## 1. Superintelligence in a few thousand days

**[Quote]**

*It is possible that we will have **superintelligence** in a few thousand days (!);*

**[Quote]**

**Take:** A bit lofty, but what do I know?

---

## 2. Deep learning, scale, and resources

**[Quote]**

*Deep learning worked, got predictably better with scale, and we dedicated increasing resources to it.*

**[Quote]**

**Take:** This speaks to the observation that **frontier models** will continue to need bigger, faster, and more expensive supercomputers to improve.

---

## 3. An algorithm that learns any distribution

**[Quote]**

*Humanity discovered an algorithm that could really, truly learn any distribution of data (or really, the underlying “rules” that produce any distribution of data).*

**[Quote]**

**Take:** The claim is that the core algorithm scales: same recipe, more compute and data, better generalization. That’s the bet behind the scaling-laws story and the race for frontier-scale infrastructure.

---

## Closing

Altman’s post ties deep learning’s success to predictable gains with scale and to the idea that we’ve found an algorithm that can learn any distribution. The timeline (“a few thousand days” to superintelligence) is bold; the implication for frontier models—bigger, faster, more expensive supercomputers—is the operational takeaway for anyone building or planning infrastructure.
